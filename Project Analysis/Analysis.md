# Farmers Market Casualties – Analysis & Insights

## Overview
This analysis summarizes key insights derived from the dashboard built on
casualty data. The objective is to understand **severity levels, vehicle types,
road conditions, area type, time trends, and environmental factors** contributing
to casualties.

---

## Primary KPIs
- **Total Casualties:** 1,422
- Casualties are classified into three severity levels:
  - **Slight:** 1,151 (majority of cases)
  - **Serious:** 229
  - **Fatal:** 42

This indicates that most incidents result in non-fatal injuries, while
fatal cases form a small but critical portion.

---

## Vehicle-wise Analysis
- **Cars** account for the highest number of casualties (1,091),
  making them the primary contributor.
- Other vehicles such as **Bikes (119)**, **Goods Vans (114)**,
  and **Buses (78)** contribute significantly less.
- Agricultural vehicles contribute a negligible share.

**Insight:** Road safety interventions should prioritize car-related incidents,
as they dominate casualty figures.

---

## Road Type Analysis
- **Single Carriageway** roads show the highest number of casualties.
- **Dual Carriageways** contribute a smaller share.
- **Roundabouts, Slip Roads, and One Way Streets** have relatively low impact.

**Insight:** Single carriageway roads pose higher risk and require focused
traffic management and safety measures.

---

## Road Surface Condition
- **Dry roads** account for the majority of casualties.
- **Wet roads** contribute a noticeable share.
- **Snow and other conditions** have minimal impact.

**Insight:** High casualties on dry roads suggest that traffic volume and
speed may be more influential than adverse weather alone.

---

## Area-wise Distribution
- **Rural areas** report significantly higher casualties (~1.0k)
  compared to **Urban areas** (~0.4k).

**Insight:** Rural roads may lack safety infrastructure, emergency response,
or controlled traffic systems, increasing casualty risk.

---

## Light & Weather Conditions
- Casualties are almost evenly split between **Daylight** and **Darkness**.
- Weather-related casualties are largely categorized under **Fog / Other**,
with limited variation across conditions.

**Insight:** Visibility conditions play a role, but casualties occur consistently
across light and weather conditions, indicating the need for all-time safety measures.

---

## Time-Based (Monthly) Trend
- Casualties peak during **February and November**.
- Mid-year months show relatively lower recorded casualties.

**Insight:** Seasonal patterns may influence casualty rates, possibly due to
traffic density, weather changes, or travel behavior during specific months.

---

## Key Takeaways
- Casualties are heavily concentrated in **slight severity** cases.
- **Cars, rural areas, and single carriageway roads** are the most critical
  contributors.
- Environmental conditions alone do not explain casualty patterns;
  infrastructure and traffic behavior are major factors.

---

## Business / Policy Implications
- Focus road safety campaigns on car drivers and rural road networks.
- Improve infrastructure and monitoring on single carriageway roads.
- Strengthen preventive measures during high-risk months.
- Enhance safety controls regardless of weather or light conditions.

---

## Conclusion
The analysis highlights clear concentration areas for casualties.
Targeted interventions based on **vehicle type, road type, and area**
can significantly improve road safety outcomes.
